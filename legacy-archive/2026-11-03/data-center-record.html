Verified chronology

The process changed materially after May 13.

The sequence below uses official minutes, official recordings, Planning Commission packets, moratorium drafts, and County consultant materials. Where a document is missing, the limitation is stated. Click any event for evidence details.

May 13, 2026

90-day moratorium adopted

The BOCC adopted a 90-day moratorium by motion, 3–2, covering Project Bluestem and other data-center, battery-storage, and crypto-related proposals while regulations and additional information were considered.

Verified action
June 10

Draft regulatory framework continues

BOCC materials included a revised data-center regulation draft and separate draft moratoria. Official minutes show no action on those separate June moratorium drafts.

Primary documents
July 8

Planning Commission work session

The Planning Commission worked through data-center and BESS standards including zoning pathways, development agreements, closed-loop cooling, setbacks, public sound monitoring, third-party inspections, and developer-funded improvements.

Official PC record
July 22

Kutak Rock authorized for specialized County legal work

County records show authorization of Kutak Rock for legal services related to the proposed data-center development, with cost to be paid from Cloverleaf-provided funds. The executed engagement agreement was not publicly located.

County professional work
Aug. 5

Moratorium extension fails 2–3

A motion to extend the data-center moratorium through January 31 failed. County staff then indicated the existing moratorium would expire.

Verified action
Aug. 12

Planning Commission tables DEV-26-100, 8–1

At the public hearing, the Planning Commission tabled the proposed language amendments until the next month's meeting and requested a joint BOCC/Planning Commission work session. Andrew Gribble cast the no vote. Staff also stated there was no formal data-center application before the Commission that night.

Verified action
Aug. 18

Project proposal reportedly submitted

The next day's official BOCC recording states that a project proposal had “came forward yesterday.” The public archive review did not locate the date-stamped application package or a public case number.

Official statement; document missing
Aug. 19

New moratorium draft tabled after reported filing

Unsigned draft Resolution 2026-14 was in the packet. During the official recording, County officials said the new filing changed the legal posture, and the Board tabled the moratorium item. The draft Aug. 19 minutes later embedded in the Aug. 26 packet omit this discussion.

Evidence map

What is established — and what is not.

This is the site's central distinction. Each category has a different evidentiary weight.

Verified public record

  • May 13 moratorium adopted 3–2.
  • Aug. 5 extension failed 2–3.
  • Aug. 12 Planning Commission tabled DEV-26-100 8–1.
  • Four distinct regulation versions are preserved: April/May, June, July, and August.
  • The County used a developer-funded mechanism for third-party review and professional work.
Basis: BOCC minutes/recordings, Planning Commission packets/recordings, Cost Funding Agreement, consultant authorization records.

County / consultant analysis

  • Stifel modeled a conceptual ±1,145.5-acre project and a 1.2 GW electrical load.
  • Stifel's report is explicitly a preliminary analysis of gross public-sector revenues.
  • SMH proposed $99,650 in Phase 1 roadway engineering in the anticipated development area.
  • These are analyses and planning inputs, not final approvals or guaranteed outcomes.
Basis: August 19 BOCC packet; Stifel report; SMH proposal.

Representations & public testimony

  • Residents raised recurring concerns about water, power, noise, emissions, property impacts, rural character, incentives, and independent review.
  • Project/utility-related figures discussed in meetings are labeled as attributed representations unless backed by the underlying document.
  • Public testimony is preserved as part of the record but is not treated as proof of every factual assertion made.
Basis: BOCC and Planning Commission recordings/transcripts.

Not established in the posted record

  • The formal Aug. 18 application package and public case number.
  • A final site plan, final power configuration, or final water demand.
  • An executed Project Bluestem development agreement, PILOT, tax-abatement, or community-benefit agreement.
  • The underlying Evergy calculation cited for the 1.2 GW electricity estimate.
  • A project-specific Suburban Water will-serve/capacity letter.
Basis: primary-document source-gap audit through Aug. 25, 2026.
Fiscal record

Stifel modeled gross revenue — not net public benefit.

That distinction is essential. Project-related governmental costs were not quantified in the report, and several major inputs remain assumptions or were supported by underlying materials that were not publicly posted.

Do not read these as guaranteed outcomes. Stifel describes its work as preliminary gross public-sector revenue analysis. The annexation scenario is heavily affected by modeled Tonganoxie electricity sales-tax and franchise-fee assumptions. The public archive did not contain the underlying cost/benefit workbook, a final project application, or executed incentive/community-benefit agreements.
Developer-funded review

What the public documents actually show.

The County's public record shows a mechanism for the developer side to fund County third-party work. The posted agreement itself is unsigned and undated, so this site distinguishes the posted instrument from proof of execution.

Cost Funding Agreement

The publicly posted agreement names Western Meadowlark ProjectCo LLC and Leavenworth County, establishes an initial $450,000 fund for eligible third-party expenses, and caps aggregate contributions at $500,000 without developer consent.

Public version located in the May 6 BOCC packet; executed copy not publicly located.

Professional work

County records later show Kutak Rock authorization for specialized legal work and an SMH roadway-engineering proposal. The record should be read as County-retained professional work paid from developer-provided funds — not as proof that those professionals represented the developer.
