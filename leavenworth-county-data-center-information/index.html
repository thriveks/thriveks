Developments since the August archive

Update reviewed November 3, 2026. These entries extend—rather than overwrite—the original May–August record. Developer claims and independent meeting summaries are identified as such.

Sep. 28, 2026

Power-generation application withdrawn

Cloverleaf announced that it withdrew the original application for a power-generation facility while evaluating alternative locations. The developer said Project Bluestem's data-center rezoning application remained active. Read source.

Data-center regulations returned for another hearing

The Board of County Commissioners voted 4–1 to return the proposed Planned Industrial Technology District regulations to the Planning Commission for additional review, addressing nuclear-power provisions, data-center size categories, noise measurements, and reclamation. Read source.

County minutes reproduced in secondary archive

BESS recommendations advanced; data-center rules deferred

The Planning Commission recommended battery-energy-storage regulations to the County Commission. It deferred separate data-center regulation language for later consideration. Read source.

Battery-storage amendments adopted, not project approval

The County Commission adopted Resolution 2026-22 on standalone battery-energy-storage regulation. This action did not approve Project Bluestem or establish its data-center-specific rules. Read source.

Commissioners voted 3–2 to ask staff to prepare a proposal for a countywide advisory-only data-center vote. This directed staff work; it did not itself schedule a vote or decide the project. Read source.

Fiscal record

Stifel modeled gross revenue — not net public benefit.

That distinction is essential. Project-related governmental costs were not quantified in the report, and several major inputs remain assumptions or were supported by underlying materials that were not publicly posted.

Developer-funded review

What the public documents actually show.

The County's public record shows a mechanism for the developer side to fund County third-party work. The posted agreement itself is unsigned and undated, so this site distinguishes the posted instrument from proof of execution.

Cost Funding Agreement

The publicly posted agreement names Western Meadowlark ProjectCo LLC and Leavenworth County, establishes an initial $450,000 fund for eligible third-party expenses, and caps aggregate contributions at $500,000 without developer consent.

Public version located in the May 6 BOCC packet; executed copy not publicly located.

BESS vs. data centers

A real structural difference in the draft rules.

The August draft contains more explicit lifecycle protections for battery energy storage systems than for data centers. Staff explained that the regulatory pathways differ; critics argued comparable protections should be applied to the larger use.

BESS draft

  • Decommissioning agreement
  • Abandonment provisions
  • Financial security
  • Emergency-response requirements
  • Transfer approval and fee
  • Insurance / indemnification provisions

Data-center draft

The data-center framework relies more heavily on rezoning/development-plan review and project-specific development agreements for performance standards, penalties, and site-specific conditions.

Important: “Permitted by right” in a PIT district does not mean permitted anywhere in the County without review. The land would first have to obtain the applicable zoning and proceed through the required development-plan/public process.

Why publish now?

The public record is already sufficient to document the County's formal actions, the regulatory timeline, consultant analyses, funding structure, and the limits of what has been publicly posted. Missing documents are identified as missing rather than filled in with assumptions. That makes the record useful now while remaining updateable as new official material is posted.
