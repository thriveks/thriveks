LOCAL ECONOMY

What helps local businesses grow—and what keeps them from it?

Research theme · ongoing

Thrive looks at the practical barriers that shape a community's economy: access to business space, financing, tools, workforce, transportation and predictable development processes. We ask how those barriers affect owners already here, not only companies being recruited.

The goal is to translate broad economic-development ambitions into questions that can be tested and addressed.

PUBLIC RECORDS & PERFORMANCE

How should we measure economic-development results?

Public-source reviews · analysis

One area of our research compares reported leads, active prospects, announced projects, jobs and completed investments. These numbers describe different stages. Treating them as interchangeable can obscure what public and private resources have accomplished.

We also examine the cost and scope of development programs and compare reporting methods with peer communities. Fair comparisons need matching time periods, populations, service areas and definitions.

Read our LCDC research example and supporting sources

Leavenworth County Development Corporation (LCDC) is one of several organizations and public-data topics examined in this work. Its 2025 annual report describes 50 inquiries, 13 active opportunities at year-end, nine new jobs and 38 jobs retained through existing business support. It also discusses potential projects; prospects are not the same as completed outcomes. The publicly reported figures are attributed to LCDC, not independently audited here.

Questions include project-level attribution, realized tax-base effects, geographic distribution of outcomes, and consistent comparison with peers. This is independent analysis, not a claim of wrongdoing or an official government position.

INFRASTRUCTURE

What would better freight access change?

Exploratory research

Rail and transload work examines shipping costs, potential business demand, site conditions, operator requirements and funding paths. Those questions matter because transport options can influence how companies evaluate a community. No rail or transload project is presented as approved or engineered.

A previously published study is part of the evidence base, but updated demand and feasibility work would be necessary for any serious investment decision.

REDEVELOPMENT

What makes a site ready to use again?

Independent planning concepts

Vacancy alone does not make redevelopment feasible. Ownership, title, infrastructure, flood risk, building condition, financing, market demand and the needs of current users can all become constraints. Our work explores structured ways to evaluate sites without making premature commitments.

YOUTH & OPPORTUNITY

How can more young people build a future here?

Program exploration

Youth-retention and workforce ideas consider entrepreneurship, practical technical skills, local business opportunities, and connections between employers, educators and young people. These are areas of program design, not claims that a new initiative has already launched.

HOW THIS LIBRARY WORKS

Readable. Traceable. Correctable.

For each major report, we aim to identify the source, date, geography, method, assumptions, and unknowns. We distinguish proposed outcomes from completed ones, and independent analysis from official records. Research should be challenged when better information becomes available.

Have a correction, relevant record or additional source? Contact Thrive. Unpublished working documents are not automatically cleared for public release; the current site provides public-source references.
